\section{How We Collected and Read the Literature}
\label{app:method}

\paragraph{Search.} We combined four sources, first accessed on November 23--24, 2026:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item the arXiv API, queried for ``weight-space programming'', ``weight space programming'' and related phrases (``edit its own weights'', ``writes the weights'', ``LLM edits weights'');
\item the arXiv-hosted entries of a community-maintained list \citep{savoji2026awesome};
\item the released corpus file of Türkoğlu's survey \citep{turkoglu2026survey}, used only to find candidate papers;
\item citations from papers already included (snowballing).
\end{enumerate}
The first round (November 23--24) produced \nFirstRound\ candidate records: arXiv preprints and Zenodo records with a paper or report. GitHub-only projects, blog posts and social-media threads are not included, though many are listed in \citet{savoji2026awesome}.

\paragraph{Update search.} On November 25, 2026 we reran the same sources. The arXiv API was queried with the phrases above and with ten keyword queries restricted to submissions from November 2 to 25 (for example weights together with ``gradient-free'', ``without gradient'' or ``weight edit'', and an LLM or agent). We also compared the arXiv and Zenodo identifiers in the corpus file of \citet{turkoglu2026survey} and in a second community list with ours. This identified \nUpdateIdentified\ records not already in the corpus. Of these, \nUpdateTitleExcluded\ were excluded on title and abstract, most because they matched a keyword but did not involve a model reading, writing or editing network weights; \nUpdateMerged\ were alternate identifiers of records already read; and \nUpdateFullText\ were read in full. Every query, its hit count, and every exclusion with a one-line reason are released with the corpus. In total, \nScreened\ records were read from their full text; for \nPartialAll\ of them only part could be read, as recorded in their notes.

\paragraph{Reading.} Every record was read from its full text, by arXiv HTML or PDF or by the Zenodo record's files, using a fixed extraction form. The form records: editor model and target; interface and loop; whether the editor edits itself; headline results; cost; controls; runs and variance; robustness of edits; verification; evidence on memorization versus reasoning; stated limitations; and code. Every recorded number carries a verbatim quote and a location in the source. Where only part of a record could be read (long appendices, unreachable supplementary files), the form says so. The \nPartial\ included records with partial reads are listed in the released notes. In a few notes we added a quote after the reading pass; these additions are marked in the note. Records were coded on the four axes of \cref{sec:defs}. \nExcluded\ records were excluded as not WSP or as duplicates of another record.

\paragraph{Recount of the original edit's cost.} From the released logs of the original 50-pass Qwen run (\texttt{exp8\_math/runs/p50c}, 158 turns in four conversations), we summed input and output token usage in three ways:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item over all turns: 89.69M;
\item the last turn of each conversation: 5.59M;
\item plateau-aware: 6.46M. After a harness restart in the first conversation, the cumulative counter stalls at about 348k for 26 turns, each reporting the restart state plus one call. We count the restart state once and add each stalled turn's increment over it.
\end{itemize}
The logs' README states that per-turn usage accumulates over a conversation and should not be summed over turns. The script and its output are released with the corpus at \url{https://github.com/Abelo9996/weight-space-programming-survey}.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The corpus covers a window of just over three weeks of a fast-moving literature, plus one study posted a month before the term was coined. Apart from one workshop paper \citep{ax2610_07334}, none of the included works is peer reviewed, and notes refer to the version we read, which may since have changed. Several records, on Zenodo and on arXiv, disclose that a language model wrote substantial parts of the paper, and some are AI-authored. Search recall is limited: Zenodo was not searched directly (Zenodo records come from community lists and another survey's corpus), Preprints.org, OpenReview, SSRN and blogs were not searched, phrase queries miss studies that describe WSP in other words, and the first round missed at least six in-window records that the update search found. Of the \nIncluded\ included studies, \nPeripheral\ are peripheral: baselines, evaluations, closed-form or solver studies without an LLM editor, and studies where WSP is secondary or the target is not a digital neural network, and several inclusion decisions are judgment calls. Coding was done by one reading pass per study, without blind double coding. The interface and loop codes in \cref{tab:papers} were derived by rule from the free-text fields of the notes and corrected by hand in \nCodeOverrides\ cases; the corrections are released with the corpus. We report each study's own numbers under its own protocol, and we do not attempt cross-study normalization.
