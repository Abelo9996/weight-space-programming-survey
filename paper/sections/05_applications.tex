\section{Applications}
\label{sec:apps}

Within three weeks, WSP had been tried in more than ten application areas. \Cref{tab:apps} summarizes them. For each area, the table gives the studies, the best-controlled result we found and the main caveat that comes with it. Two patterns recur across areas. First, WSP works best where the failure is \emph{concentrated}, in one direction, one embedding row or one unit, and where a known rule can be written down. Second, in almost every area with a strong cheap baseline, that baseline matches most of the WSP gain. This includes a closed-form statistic, a script written after the fact, a decoding change or a small fine-tune.

\paragraph{Language models.} The largest cluster repairs small open LLMs. Its largest single target is Qwen3.5-0.8B, the target of the original edit (\cref{sec:repair}). The setting is attractive because the failure, looping until the token limit, is concentrated and easy to measure. That same property limits what the results show about WSP in general. Beyond 0.8B, edits have been reported on Qwen3.5-9B \citep{ax2611_09382}, Gemma-4-12B \citep{zen24156363}, a 4B Gemma in a benchmark \citep{ax2611_08647}, and 27B models editing themselves \citep{ax2611_21885,ax2611_29513,ax2611_31117}. Repairs of larger models have so far had smaller effects, while backdoor implants work at full strength on 1B to 4B models \citep{ax2611_27920,ax2611_27425}.

\paragraph{Robotics and embodied agents.} Robot policies were among the first non-LLM targets. On large vision-language-action models, the successful edits so far are lexical remappings or closed-form instruction distillation, often in embedding rows \citep{ax2611_09376,ax2611_11894}. On a 3.35B foundation policy, an agent-written edit that triggers a corrective movement from a recognizable stuck state raised success on three simulated tasks and gave no significant improvement on two others, while random edits of the same size made no consistent difference \citep{ax2611_31715}. Small controllers can be written from zero and transferred to a real tractor \citep{ax2611_21958}, and policies for discrete MDPs can be written and formally verified \citep{ax2611_11604}.

\paragraph{Production systems.} The first industrial report in our corpus applies agentic weight editing to a production recommender, with held-out days and an online A/B test \citep{ax2611_29613}. A GUI-agent study builds a closed-form edit from trajectory feedback and evaluates it on OSWorld \citep{ax2611_32332}. Neither reports a random-edit control; Weight Coding evaluates on held-out days and with an online A/B test.

\paragraph{Clinical and biomedical models.} Several clinical studies planted known flaws, included healthy controls, preregistered and blinded their analyses, and compared against simple weight statistics and black-box query audits \citep{ax2611_05341,ax2611_07847,ax2611_24877,zen24040591}. Their common conclusion is that WSP can find and fix a flaw that is visible in a single unit. It cannot yet certify that a network is correct, and black-box computation matches white-box reading \citep{ax2611_07847}. Biosignal and medical-imaging studies apply writing mode. A written EEG network beat the same architecture trained on 5 subjects and matched training on 30 on one task (0.834 against 0.806), but fell below it on another (0.769) \citep{ax2611_13758}, while written MedMNIST classifiers beat a zero-shot vision-language model on 5 of 6 tasks but lost to training on 256 labels \citep{ax2611_12066}.

\paragraph{Science.} Scientific applications use WSP to inject known structure (physical laws, material descriptors, Hamiltonian structure) or to turn experimental feedback into persistent updates \citep{ax2611_12954,ax2611_22013,ax2611_11537,ax2611_07319}. The particle-physics study is a useful template. It compares an agent edit, removal of sparse-autoencoder latents, and fine-tuning on the same fault, and evaluates on downstream heads not used to build the edit \citep{ax2611_27079}.

\paragraph{Security.} Security is the clearest dual-use area. Backdoor implantation is cheap, selective and stealthy \citep{ax2611_17395,ax2611_27920,ax2611_27425}. Detection by weight statistics works against careless edits. A per-neuron norm scan is defeated by norm matching, though the norm-matched implant was not re-tested behaviourally \citep{zen24156363}, and a trigger-recovery statistic survives freezing and camouflage but not an attacker that freezes and fills the scanned block \citep{ax2611_19304}. We return to this in \cref{sec:rsi-safety}.
